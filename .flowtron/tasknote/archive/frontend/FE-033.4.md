### Clarifying-question outcomes (resolved 2026-05-17)

User picked A on both questions surfaced via AskUserQuestion.

**Q1 — Section-tint structural form.** Chose **A (Default's full-saturation form — `bg-{hue}-50 border-{hue}-200 dark:bg-{hue}-950/30 dark:border-{hue}-900`).** Rationale: (a) honors FE-033.1 Discovery's "more saturated than Linear" framing — saturated bg + saturated border carries GitHub's "saturated accent pops on charcoal" character; (b) differentiates the 3rd palette from Linear (slate-bg + accent border) and from Default (warm hues) — Default = warm-saturated, Linear = cool-restrained, GitHub = cool-saturated with purple/blue character; (c) the purple-magenta gradient pops harder against a saturated bg than slate bg would carry.

**Q2 — Priority hue strategy.** Chose **A (Discovery's purple-magenta gradient + cool blue — Critical=fuchsia, High=purple, Medium=violet, Low=blue, Future=cyan, Completed=emerald).** Rationale: (a) directly implements FE-033.1 line 123's locked framing; (b) biggest visible delta vs Default (5 hue shifts: Critical/High/Medium/Low/Future change; Completed preserved per universal-done); (c) the fuchsia→purple→violet gradient honors GitHub's iconic "merged-PR purple" voice across the highest-attention slots; cool blue for Low and cyan for Future extend the cool family into low-urgency space.

**ROW_HIGHLIGHT vs PHASE_DOT.active:** yellow vs blue — distinct families (the FE-033.1 amber-collision fix made manifest: highlight in one family, active-state in another).

**ROW_SELECTION pink vs all priority section borders:** pink ≠ fuchsia/purple/violet/blue/cyan/emerald. No clash.
